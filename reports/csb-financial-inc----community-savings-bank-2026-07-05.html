Verdict: Community Savings Bank is a very small, rural Ohio thrift with a dangerously elevated loan-to-deposit ratio of 113.5% and heavy reliance on brokered deposits and FHLB advances. Asset quality is a bright spot at 0.11% NPLs, but the bank's thin earnings, deposit structure weaknesses, and outsized consumer loan growth from third-party purchases raise meaningful concerns. This conversion appears to be primarily a capital-raising exercise to reduce expensive funding rather than a story of operational strength.
At $110.2 million in total assets, CSB Financial is an extremely small thrift conversion that will trade with very limited liquidity post-IPO. The 113.5% loan-to-deposit ratio is the most glaring concern — it sits well above the 80-90% sweet spot and signals the bank is funding loans with expensive wholesale sources (brokered CDs and FHLB advances) rather than organic deposit growth. Core deposits represent only 42.5% of total deposits, a structurally weak funding profile that compresses margins and creates rate sensitivity. The bank swung from a $369K loss in 2024 to $439K net income in 2025, but the 2024 loss was partially attributed to one-time core conversion costs — meaning underlying earnings power is thin and barely proven. The rapid growth in unsecured consumer loans purchased from a third party — jumping from $2.6M to $17.5M in a single year — introduces credit risk that has not yet seasoned through an economic cycle. Until deposit franchise quality improves materially and the consumer loan book seasons without deterioration, this conversion warrants caution despite the attractive NPL ratio.
