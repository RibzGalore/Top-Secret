Verdict: Community Savings Bank is a very small Ohio mutual thrift with a troubling loan-to-deposit ratio of 113.5%, heavy reliance on brokered deposits and FHLB advances, and a razor-thin earnings profile. While asset quality is pristine and the conversion will inject needed capital, the bank's core deposit franchise is underdeveloped and the business model carries meaningful funding risk.
At $110.2 million in total assets, this is a micro-thrift that barely registers on the regional bank radar, which creates both opportunity and risk for conversion investors. The most glaring concern is the 113.5% loan-to-deposit ratio, which signals the bank is funding loan growth with expensive wholesale money — brokered CDs and FHLB advances — rather than organic core deposits. Core deposits represent only 42.5% of total deposits, a structurally weak funding base that pressures NIM and makes the bank vulnerable to rate cycles. Net income of just $439,000 in 2025 after a $369,000 loss in 2024 reflects a barely-break-even operation; the 2024 loss, while partially explained by one-time core conversion costs, highlights how thin the margin for error is. The surge in unsecured consumer loan purchases from a third party (from $2.6M to $17.5M in one year) is a yellow-to-red flag — rapid growth in purchased unsecured loans at a small community thrift introduces credit and concentration risk that can deteriorate quickly in a downturn. On the positive side, reported NPLs of 0.11% are exceptional, and the IPO proceeds will meaningfully recapitalize the institution, but investors should be cautious until the bank demonstrates sustained profitability and deposit franchise improvement.
