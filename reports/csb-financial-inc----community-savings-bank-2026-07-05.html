Verdict: Community Savings Bank is a tiny, single-market Ohio thrift with a dangerous loan-to-deposit ratio of 113.5%, heavy reliance on brokered deposits and FHLB advances, and a very thin core deposit base (only 42.5% of deposits). The bank barely returned to profitability in 2025 after a net loss in 2024, and the franchise is extremely small with limited scale advantages. While asset quality is pristine and the conversion structure is standard, the funding risk and operational fragility make this a high-caution situation.
At $110.2 million in total assets, Community Savings Bank is one of the smallest thrift conversions to come to market, with a two-office footprint serving rural Clermont and Highland Counties in Ohio. The most glaring concern is the 113.5% loan-to-deposit ratio — well above the safe 80-90% range — which the bank itself acknowledges is funded by brokered CDs and FHLB advances, both expensive and unstable funding sources. Core deposits represent only 42.5% of total deposits ($34.9M), meaning the bank is structurally dependent on hot money to fund its loan book. The consumer loan portfolio exploded from $2.6M to $17.5M in a single year through third-party purchases of unsecured loans, which introduces credit model risk that is difficult to assess from the prospectus alone. On the positive side, non-performing loans are essentially zero at 0.11%, and the conversion proceeds are explicitly earmarked to reduce reliance on brokered funding — a credible use of capital. However, the bank earned only $439K in 2025 on a $110M asset base, implying an ROA well below 0.50%, and the 2024 net loss (even if one-time-driven) underscores fragile earnings power. Investors should demand a meaningful discount to tangible book value to compensate for the funding risk, tiny scale, and earnings volatility before committing capital.
